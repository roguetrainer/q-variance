# Summary of "Q-Variance: or, a Duet Concerning the Two Chief World Systems" by Orrell & Wilmott

The article, presented as a **dialogue** (a "duet") to evoke Galileo's famous work, discusses the concept of **Q-Variance**—a property of asset price behavior derived from applying quantum theory to finance.

To experiment with the Q-Variance model try the Shiny app **qvar** https://david-systemsforecasting.shinyapps.io/qvar/.

---

## The Core Concept: Q-Variance

Q-variance is a key finding that links an asset's expected **variance (volatility)** over a period of time to the **price change** that occurred during that period.

### 1. The Relationship

Unlike classical financial models (like Black-Scholes) which assume variance is independent of the price change, Q-variance is defined by a simple **quadratic relationship**.

The expected variance, $V(z)$, of log returns $x$ over a period $T$ is approximated by:

$$
V(z) = \sigma^2 + \frac{z^2}{2}
$$

Where $z = x/\sqrt{T}$ (the scaled price displacement), and $\sigma$ is a base volatility parameter.

### 2. Origins

The property was initially a **prediction** made by Orrell using a **quantum oscillator model**. The "Q" in Q-Variance can stand for both **Quadratic** (referring to the formula) and **Quantum** (referring to its theoretical source).

### 3. Empirical Evidence

The authors demonstrate that this predicted quadratic relationship is **empirically supported** by real-world financial data across various asset types, making the model a significant departure from traditional financial theory.

---

## Key Implications

The article explores the consequences of Q-Variance for modern finance, including:

* **Asset Price Modeling:** It suggests a new, more accurate framework for modeling asset prices that moves beyond the classical random walk.
* **Implied Volatility:** It leads to the development of a **Quantum Implied Volatility (QIV) model**, which can be used to better price options and understand market expectations of risk.
* **Risk Estimation:** The findings provide a novel approach to estimating and managing financial risk by recognizing the intrinsic link between price movement and subsequent volatility.
